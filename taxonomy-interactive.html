    3 · Model robustness

    Sample size, performance, and what a display leaves out

    A single performance number hides how sure we are about it and whether it still holds after deployment. Both demonstrations start from what Jauk et al. reported: an AUROC of 0.854 on the test data and 0.855 during seven months of prospective use, while calibration deteriorated.

    Illustrative risk distribution, not data from the study. Overstatement is modeled as a constant shift on the log-odds scale, which leaves the ranking of patients unchanged, and with it the AUROC and the percentile-based icons (red for the 95th to 100th percentile, yellow for the 85th to 94th).

      Report performance with its uncertainty and the size of the evaluation behind it, and keep checking calibration after deployment. A display that encodes only rank cannot show drift, so reliability has to be communicated, not only measured.

      4 · What if

      What if the output does not get full attention?

      Alerts compete with interruptions, time pressure, and other alarms. Drag attention toward “rushed” and switch off what the display communicates. The less attention there is, the more the display has to carry on its own, and the more support the decision needs.
